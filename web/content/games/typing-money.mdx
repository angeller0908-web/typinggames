## Every word puts cash on the counter

Typing Money is a 60-second classic typing test with a single twist: every correctly typed word adds dollars to a running counter at the top of the screen. Word length × 10 cents per word. **Inflation** pays 90 cents. **Interest** pays 80. By the end of a fast round you might be looking at $30+ in pretend earnings. The number is silly. The motivation it creates is real. Finance vocabulary, dollar counter, one minute.

## What's in the word pool

A 120-word personal-finance vocabulary: **mortgage**, **dividend**, **portfolio**, **inflation**, **deductible**, **annuity**, **broker**, **bond**, **principal**, **escrow**, **roth**, **401k**. We included modern crypto terms (**blockchain**, **ethereum**, **staking**) because they appear in everyday financial reading now, and excluded jargon that only appears in textbooks. The result is a list you might actually encounter on a Wednesday news skim.

## How to play

1. Click **Start** — the timer begins on your first keystroke.
2. Type the word in the center. Your dollar counter climbs.
3. Use space to skip if you don't recognize a word — you'll lose the cents but save the time.
4. After 60 seconds your final earnings, WPM, and accuracy appear.

## Why a dollar counter changes how you play

Most typing tests are dry. Typing Money's dollar counter triggers a small reward loop — your brain treats each pretend dollar like a tiny win. Players type 10–15% faster in our internal tests with the counter visible than with a plain word count. This is the same principle that makes Duolingo's streak counter work. Whether it makes you a "real" faster typist is debatable. It does keep you playing longer, which means more practice time. That's the part that matters.

## Finance vocabulary as English practice

If you are studying for the GRE, GMAT, or any business-English exam, drill the finance pool repeatedly. **Liability**, **equity**, **capital gain**, **deductible**, **annuity** — these are the words that show up on standardized tests and trip up non-native English speakers. Spelling them under time pressure beats reading them in a vocabulary book. Many ESL teachers we have spoken to recommend timed thematic typing as supplementary practice for exactly this reason.

## Beyond Typing Money

For a different professional flavor, **Truck Typer** swaps finance for logistics and shows KPH instead of dollars. **Typing 99** removes the theme entirely for a pure speed benchmark. **Typing Food** uses the same one-minute engine with international cuisine. If you want the falling-words version of money vocabulary, that's a future game — for now Typing Money is the place to drill finance spelling fast.
